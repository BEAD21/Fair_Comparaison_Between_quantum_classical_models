Classical filters in the comparison

The classical half of the fair comparison. Every model below replaces only the filter of the hybrid QCNN: encoding options, normalisation, activation, pooling and the 258-parameter head are identical, and each model got the same 12-recipe grid, dev crops (Cherry, Grape), seeds and selection rule as the quantum filters. The quantum model is the same pipeline with the orange box replaced by: amplitude encoding → 2-qubit circuit → ⟨Z₀⟩, ⟨Z₁⟩.

Two input versions: raw and amp

raw: the filter sees the grey pixel values as they are.

amp: before the filter, each non-overlapping 2×2 block is replaced by |x| / ‖x‖, exactly what the quantum amplitude encoding does to a patch. No parameters. So the amp version gets the same input as the circuit; the only difference left is the filter itself.

Effect: the absolute brightness of each block is removed, only its pattern remains. For the 3×3 models a window covers parts of up to four normalised blocks.

Every total is filter + 258 head: 270, except Conv2x2 (268). B6 (quantum) for reference: 12 + 258 = 270, validation 86.90. Validation = mean last-5-epoch accuracy, 7 crops, seeds 100–109.

Why these five. A fair counterpart must answer "could a classical piece of the same size do the circuit's job?". PatchMLP answers it most strictly (same patches, same count, nonlinear like the circuit). Conv2x2 is the plain convolution on the same patches. The three 3×3 models show what happens when the 12 parameters are spent on a larger, overlapping view of the image instead, each with a different way of reaching exactly 12.

Not in this comparison

PaperTwin (20 params: 2 dense 3×3 kernels + 2 biases): the classical twin used in an earlier version of this work. Not parameter-matched, so not used.
Pool2 (0 params: 2×2 average and 2×2 max as the 2 channels): the classical "no filter" baseline of the earlier final runs; the matching quantum control here is q/identity.
